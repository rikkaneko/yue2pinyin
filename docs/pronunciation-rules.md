# Stage 1 pronunciation rules

Before any Jyutping lookup, the input is split into text units and then into nonseparator spans and individual separators. Each contiguous Latin word is one unit; other characters remain individual units. Separators are Unicode punctuation or whitespace, including ASCII and fullwidth forms; Unicode symbols are processed as ordinary text. Longest matching runs only within a nonseparator span, so no dictionary word crosses a separator. Rime readings take precedence over Words.hk headword readings from `YEN2PINYIN_FLASHCARD_WORDS_PATH`. Latin lookup ignores case while responses preserve input case. A matching Cantonese word receives one Jyutping syllable per character; a Latin word may have several syllables in its one unit. Multiple readings are scored with character counts from `charlist.json`; equal scores use source order. A character without a word match uses its highest-count character reading, with JSON source order breaking ties. Flashcard headwords whose readings cannot be aligned to units are skipped.

Both text routes expose the same lookup as parallel text-unit arrays and grouped arrays. `words` preserves matched dictionary words; fallback units, punctuation, and whitespace are individual groups. Multi-syllable Latin readings are nested arrays in `jyutpin` and `jyutpin_words`, while ordinary Cantonese word groups remain space-joined strings. `/approx_pinyin` returns corresponding nested values in `approx_pinyin`, `approx_pinyin_words`, and `hint`; an unsupported Latin syllable contributes `null` at its position. A grouped Cantonese approximation remains `null` when any unit is unsupported. The frontend labels Latin syllable hints with the corresponding approximate pinyin, and Cantonese hints with their character.

`POST /approx_pinyin` also accepts `{ "jyutpin": ["nei5", ["so1", "wi4"], null] }`. This form returns only parallel `jyutpin`, `approx_pinyin`, and `hint` arrays, with one slot per input element. Existing whitespace-separated strings such as `"so1 wi4"` remain valid and are returned as nested arrays. Explicit arrays retain their shape even with one syllable. Unsupported syllables have null approximation and hint at their own position; empty or null elements have null approximation and hint. The text and direct-input shapes cannot be mixed.

Approximate pinyin follows [`粵普拼音對照.md`](../粵普拼音對照.md) where the two guides disagree, then [`粵拼轉近似普通話拼音完整靜態映射表.md`](../粵拼轉近似普通話拼音完整靜態映射表.md). The six Jyutping tones map to Mandarin tone digits `1, 2, 1, 3, 2, 4`. The guide's optional fourth-tone alternative for Jyutping tone 3 is resolved to tone 1. A stop final `-p`, `-t`, or `-k` becomes an apostrophe immediately before the tone digit, as in `baat3 → ba'1`.

The conversion uses the guide's explicit example syllables where its general initial and final rows offer competing results, then a fixed final table for other syllables. For example, `hoeng1 → xiang1`, `seon3 → xin1`, and `syut3 → xue'1`. The approximation describes a sound cue; it is not a Mandarin dictionary reading of the character. Unsupported or malformed syllables have `null` approximation and hint. Special pronunciation cues include throat `h`, velar `ng`, rounded vowels or initials, lip-closing `-m`, and short stop finals.

The cache stores versioned compressed JSON, including hashes of the Rime CSV, character counts, and flashcard YAML, double-array `base` and `check` arrays, terminal syllables, and character counts. A changed source or unreadable cache is rebuilt at startup. Configure a writable cache path in restricted deployments.
